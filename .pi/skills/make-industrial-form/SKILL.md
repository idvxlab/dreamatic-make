# Industrial Design and Product Form

Organize form around tasks and use posture: placement, viewing angle, grip, reach, button/knob/display layout, connector access, transport, and maintenance. Develop concepts together with hardware envelopes and printing constraints.

Compare meaningful alternatives through volume, proportion, readability, feedback, assembly, and cost rather than decoration or color alone. Preserve directions the user has accepted or rejected.

Translate intent into actionable constraints: overall envelope, major surfaces or geometric relationships, screen and control positions, openings and seams, and surface requirements. Unverified comfort, thermal performance, and strength remain design goals.

Use consistent component IDs and coordinates across form descriptions, CAD, and assembly. Distinguish concept geometry from dimensioned mechanical design. A render does not establish fit.

The current CAD tool supports basic CSG. For complex surfaces, fillets, or material visualization, save achievable work and capability gaps. Do not silently replace an explicitly requested form with a simple box.

Before detailed structure, use make-appearance-prompting for actual concept imagery and make-appearance-review for user approval. Develop distinct silhouettes and interaction layouts with consistent camera and lighting. Refine one selected direction with real source imagery and user feedback. Keep product brief, provisional hardware envelopes, visual concepts and precise CAD distinct but linked through Context dependencies. A new appearance version requires fresh approval.
